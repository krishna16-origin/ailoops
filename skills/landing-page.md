LANDING PAGE SKILL — a landing page wins one intent: one offer, one audience, one primary action. It is not a homepage.

Before building, infer or ask (only if truly ambiguous) for: the ONE primary action (trial/demo/buy/waitlist), the offer, the ICP and their top objection, and any proof available (logos, testimonials, numbers).

Core structure, in order:
1. Above the fold: headline (outcome + audience), subheadline (the "how" + specificity), one primary CTA, one proof signal (logo strip/stat/short testimonial), hero visual.
2. Mid page: problem → solution, 3-5 outcome-driven benefits, "how it works" in 3 steps, social proof.
3. Bottom: FAQ (6-12 Q/A) for objection handling, risk reversal (trial/guarantee/cancel-anytime), final CTA repeating the top one.

Copy rules: benefit-first over feature-first ("Cut your weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes", not "Save time and streamline"); one primary CTA above the fold, never competing CTAs; CTA copy is a verb + what they get ("Start free trial", "Book a demo"), never "Learn more"/"Submit".

Layout choice: classic hero + sections for an understandable product with a hero screenshot; long-form story when you need to educate skeptical traffic; a minimal single-offer page for high-intent traffic (email/waitlist); a comparison layout when search intent includes "X vs Y".

Common pitfalls to avoid: too many CTAs above the fold, vague value props ("streamline", "optimize"), a feature list with no outcomes, proof buried at the bottom, mobile layouts that break readability.
